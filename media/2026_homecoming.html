PLOrk + Sideband: Homecoming

Sonic Lanterns
by Konrad Kaczmarek
performed by PLOrk & Sideband

The piece features a phone-based instrument that uses the IMU and compass sensors as well as network communication to coordinate musical material as the performers move about the audience. Seated around the perimeter of the audience, Harpists and Bassists perform arpeggio patterns and bass lines that are coordinated with the Lanternists through the network.

Artiforg
by Dan Iglesia
performed by PLOrk

Artiforg is a continuation of my work with mobile devices, networked parameters, and live 3D video. Previous instances had free improvisation on tablets over network control; this work, however, has a strict form, tether-only control, and real performative cues.

GhostLine
by Jeff Snyder
performed by Sideband

Ghost Line came out of two ideas. The first idea was to create a work where any motion a performer made, even minor motions like blinking an eye, would create a sonic result. The second idea was to try to do a very literal translation between image and sound – scanning through an array of image pixels and treating the brightness values as points to build an audio waveform. My experiments with putting these concepts into practice led down a very curvy path, but eventually to the piece you’ll hear tonight.

Radigue Resonators
by Ellie Cherry and Gulli Björnsson
performed by PLOrk

Radigue Resonators is an homage to the legendary electronic musician and composer Éliane Radigue. Written collaboratively by Princeton graduate composition students Ellie Cherry and Gulli Björnsson, the piece serves as a tribute to Radigue's patient appreciation for the most minuscule sounds in the world of electronic music.

In this piece, members of the laptop orchestra engage in a structured group improvisation across various instruments connected to a Max patch. Max analyzes the incoming pitches and maps them to control their respective frequency bands within a spectral EQ pfft~ patch. The concept behind this instrument is to use the instruments to excite the harmonic spectra of the audio files (e.g. have them them resonate), effectively functioning as a spectral resonator where your instrument begins to sound like the audio file itself. In Radigue Resonators this spectral EQ processes a collection of audio files featuring Radigue-esque drone textures and spoken-word recordings of Radigue.

Ellie Cherry and Gulli Björnsson pass on a special thanks to Jeff Snyder for his encouragement and for joining in with some wonderful dulcet electrosteel tones!

Waves at Whidbey
by Seth Cluett
performed by PLOrk & Sideband

Waves at Widbey is a networked laptop chamber work written for Brad Garton's retirement from Columbia University, following his long tenure as Professor of Composition and Director of the Computer Music Center. I asked Brad for a set of chords that could be performed in any order; I then encoded the notes into software. The piece uses recordings of ocean waves off the coast of Whidbey Island, where Garton lives, to derive tempo and density. The peaks of the waves were used to create probability tables that drive the ebb and flow of chord changes. The work is networked; the notes of the chords are distributed to each ensemble member, who has complete freedom to use the incoming note, duration, rest, and tempo values however they like. The work is a metaphor for Brad's teaching style: a generous method of sharing common tools so individuals can create deeply personal musical works.
